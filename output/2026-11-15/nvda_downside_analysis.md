# How low can NVDA go from $162.85?

Prices are the close of Friday 2026-11-13; data retrieved 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15. NVDA reports fiscal Q3 results on Tuesday 2026-11-17 after the close, so every figure below is a pre-earnings reading.

## Short answer

There is no hard floor. Five independent methods (price structure, valuation, the options market, earnings at risk in the filings, and historical analogs) cluster into four zones. Each zone needs something different to happen, and each has a different time horizon.

| Zone | Fall from $162.85 | Horizon | What has to happen | Market-implied odds of a close below the zone's lower edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. $146–153** | 6–10% | days to weeks | An ordinary bad reaction to Tuesday's report or to more headlines | below $148.80: about 20% by Nov 18, 31% by Dec 18 |
| **2. $129–142** | 13–21% | weeks to three months | The multiple falls to NVDA's own record low, or the market prices the loss of a major customer or an export hit | below $130: about 3% by Nov 18, 12% by Dec 18, 15% by Jan 15 |
| **3. $98–125** | 23–40% | six to twelve months | Earnings estimates are cut 20–35% and the multiple stays at trough | below $100: about 2% by Dec 18, 3% by Jan 15, 11% by June 2027 |
| **4. $65–90** | 45–60% | a year or more | Earnings halve, as they did in fiscal 2019–20 and 2022–23, with no recovery in the multiple | below $80: about 1% by Jan 15, 5% by June 2027, 10% by Dec 2027 |

The odds are what option prices imply (risk-neutral). They overstate real-world odds of decline when investors are paying up for protection, as they are now.

Two readings matter most:

- **The multiple is already near NVDA's record low.** At 20.6x trailing earnings it is in the bottom 2% of its own ten-year history; only late 2018 was lower (17.0x). Getting from zone 2 into zones 3 and 4 therefore depends mainly on earnings being cut, not on the multiple shrinking further.
- **A fine is not what the market is pricing.** All of NVDA's cash and debt securities ($56.6B) come to $2.34 a share. The company-specific leg of the fall erased $574B, which at a constant multiple equals a permanent loss of about 11% of revenue, more than all revenue from customers headquartered in China (8.2%).

Two flatterers: trailing GAAP EPS of $7.91 includes about $1.05 a share of gains on equity investments (ex-gains the trailing P/E is about 23.7x), and trailing free cash flow is only 66% of net income.

Facts from the filings that shape the deeper zones:

- Data center is 92.5% of revenue. Three direct customers were 16%, 15% and 13% of first-half revenue.
- Supply and capacity commitments are $279B, up from $95B in January, against $31.6B of inventory. A demand slip would bring charges on top of lost sales (the H20 charge in 2025 was $4.5B).
- Equity investments total $99B with $25B more committed, and guarantees run to $108.5B. First-half gains on those investments were $23.7B and can reverse through reported earnings.
- Debt is $33.4B after a $25B issue in June 2026; cash and debt securities are $56.6B.
- The only company filing on the events is the 8-K of 2026-11-12, which confirms the CEO's resignation effective 2026-11-11 and gives no reason. The FBI, DOJ and congressional items are press reports; no filing confirms an investigation of the company.
- Company guidance for the quarter being reported already assumed no data-center compute revenue from China.

### 5. Historical analogs

- **NVDA's own drawdowns.** Since 1999 it has had 13 completed closing drawdowns of 25% or more; 10 of them went beyond today's 31.9%. The five since 2016 were 27%, 37%, 38%, 56% and 66%. Applied to the $239.24 peak, 37–38% is $149–151, 56% is $105 and 66% is $80.
- **NVDA's four earlier export-control shocks.** The stock fell a further 0–20% from its day-5 close (median 6%, about $153) and regained its pre-event price within 12 to 50 sessions.
- **32 leadership-scandal and regulatory episodes at large companies.** From the day-5 close, the median further fall to the lowest close was 8% within 60 sessions ($149) and 25% within 250 sessions ($123); the bad quartile was 19% ($131) and 40% ($98). The day-5 close was within 5% of the eventual low in 41% of cases over 60 sessions but only 19% over a year.
- At 60 sessions the analog median is about what those stocks do from any random day, so the event effect shows mainly in the tail and at the one-year horizon.

## What Tuesday's report can change

- Revenue against the $108.0B guide and the $108.9B consensus; a fourth-quarter guide 10% below consensus maps to about $144.
- Whether the legal note and risk factors confirm subpoenas, inquiries, the new class actions or a loss accrual for the first time.
- Charges: inventory or purchase-commitment provisions, investigation costs, marks on equity investments.
- Whether the buyback continues ($99.3B of authorisation remained in July).
- Anything unusual about controls or timing, given the interim CEO is also the CFO.

## Limits

- Consensus is stale: this fiscal year's estimate has moved only from $9.62 to $9.28 in a week and next year's has risen. Tuesday will re-base every earnings-based floor.
- The earnings-at-risk prices hold today's multiple constant; the valuation floors hold today's earnings constant. A real downturn would move both.
- The analog set is small (32 episodes), its event dates were recalled and then validated only against the price reaction, delisted scandal cases are missing, and none combines a founder-CEO exit with an export-control inquiry at this scale.
- Option figures are from Friday's closing quotes, are risk-neutral, and will reprice on Monday.
- All news items are headlines, not article text.

This is an analysis of what stated assumptions imply, not a forecast and not investment advice.
