# How low can NVDA go from $162.85?

Prices are the close of Friday 2026-11-13; data retrieved 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15. NVDA reports fiscal Q3 results on Tuesday 2026-11-17 after the close, so every figure here is a pre-earnings reading that Tuesday will re-base.

## Short answer

There is no floor. The evidence reads best as a ladder of likelihood over different horizons, not as a set of support levels.

| Zone | Fall from $162.85 | How likely, and when | What would take it there |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$146–153** | 6–10% | The central case for the low over the next one to three months | Tuesday's implied one-standard-deviation move alone reaches $148–149; it traded at $148.80 on Thursday; chip peers giving back last week's bounce would be enough |
| **$129–142** | 13–21% | Roughly one in four by mid-January | A weak or withheld January-quarter guide; tighter export licensing; a pull-back in NVDA's own financing of customers; the multiple reaching its ten-year low |
| **$98–125** | 23–40% | Unlikely within weeks, but close to the typical twelve-month low in comparable episodes | Consensus earnings cut by a fifth or more, or the AI-spending selloff resuming |
| **$65–90** | 45–60% | A tail: about 5% by mid-2027 on option prices | Earnings halve and the market judges the decline structural |

## What the evidence says

### Valuation is low but is not a floor

- At 20.6x trailing earnings NVDA is near its ten-year low of 17.0x (December 2018). It is not a record low: the stock traded between 12.5x and 17x for much of 2011–13.
- Large chip peers troughed in 2022 at a median of about 12x trailing and 11x forward earnings.
- The forward multiple has already fallen about 32% from the October peak on essentially unchanged estimates. At a market multiple of 22x, today's price implies earnings 20% below this fiscal year's consensus and 53% below next year's. The price therefore discounts no growth beyond trailing earnings, but not an outright decline.
- Trailing GAAP EPS of $7.91 includes about $1.05 a share of gains on equity investments; without them the multiple is about 23.7x.

Multiple-only markers, with earnings held at today's level:

| Marker | Price | Fall |
|---|---|---|
| Broadcom's 2022 trough forward multiple (11.7x) | $142 | 13% |
| NVDA's ten-year-low trailing P/E (17.0x) on GAAP EPS | $134 | 18% |
| 4% trailing free-cash-flow yield | $131 | 19% |
| Same multiple on non-GAAP EPS ($7.01) | $119 | 27% |
| Qualcomm's 2022 trough forward multiple (9.5x) | $115 | 29% |
| 5% trailing free-cash-flow yield | $105 | 35% |
| NVDA's all-time-low trailing P/E (12.5x, December 2011) | $99 | 39% |
| Median trough trailing P/E of eight chip peers (11.8x) | $93 | 43% |

An in-line quarter on Tuesday raises trailing EPS (non-GAAP from $7.01 to about $8.20), which lifts the earnings-based markers by roughly 8–17%. The free-cash-flow markers rest on unusually weak cash conversion (66% of net income).

### Earnings at risk, from the filings

Revenue lost is converted to earnings at the reported 75% gross margin with operating costs fixed (each 1% of revenue is about 1.13% of EPS) and priced at today's multiples.

| Scenario | Revenue lost | Price | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Press-reported $7.5B of diverted chips, as a full year of lost sales | 2.5% | $158 | 3% |
| All revenue from customers headquartered in China and Hong Kong | 8.2% | $148 | 9% |
| Short seller's allegation that over 20% of compute sales reach China (unverified; filings contradict it) | 15.5% | $134 | 17% |
| Largest direct customer lost | 16% | $133 | 18% |
| Data-center revenue falls 20% | 18.5% | $129 | 21% |
| Data-center revenue falls 35% | 32.4% | $103 | 37% |

These overstate in one way and understate in another:

- **Overstate:** the price has already fallen. Since the 2026-11-06 close NVDA has lost $574B of value (about $710B relative to the Nasdaq-100, which rose). At a constant multiple that equals a permanent loss of 11–14% of revenue, more than all China-headquartered revenue. Lost volume could also be resold, since the company reports it is supply-constrained.
- **Understate:** they hold gross margin at 75% and treat costs as fully variable. Neither is safe, for the reasons below.

### Risks the scenarios do not capture

- **Supply commitments.** $279B of supply and capacity commitments, up from $95B in January, against $31.6B of inventory; $92B falls due in the rest of this fiscal year. Each 10% that turns excess is about $28B before tax (roughly $0.96 a share after tax), six times the 2025 H20 charge.
- **Customer financing.** NVDA holds about $91B of equity stakes across its ecosystem ($42.8B listed, $47.9B private), receivables rose $24.6B in the first half, it backstops $36B of AI-cloud capacity and guarantees up to $108.5B. If the interim leadership curtails this support, demand is affected directly.
- **The intermediary channel.** The exposure to tighter export or know-your-customer rules is not the 8.2% China-headquartered line but the channel once reported as Singapore billing (18–22% of revenue before the line was dropped). Licence delays could bite without any finding of diversion.
- **Gross margin.** Guided to 74.0% this quarter from 75.0%; each point is about 1.5% of next year's EPS.
- **Equity gains reversing.** First-half gains were $23.7B. A 30% markdown of the listed holdings would cut about $0.44 a share and could make GAAP earnings miss on in-line operations.
- **Share loss.** In the week NVDA fell 12.7%, AMD and Broadcom rose about 18–19% and Micron and Applied Materials 11–13%.
- **The sector leg resuming.** NVDA fell 22% in the AI-spending selloff before any company news; its beta is 2.25.
- **Estimate cuts have not started.** Thirteen downgrades came with almost no change to earnings estimates.
- **A fine is not the risk.** All cash and debt securities ($56.6B) is $2.34 a share. Licence conditions, which hit revenue, are what matter.

### History

- **NVDA's own drawdowns.** Of 13 closing drawdowns of 25% or more since 1999, 10 went beyond today's 31.9%. The five since 2016 were 27%, 37%, 38%, 56% and 66%; applied to the $239.24 peak, 37–38% is $149–151, 56% is $105 and 66% is $80. Those started from far higher multiples (32–58x) in every case except 2018.
- **NVDA's four earlier export-control shocks.** A further fall of 0–20% from the day-5 close (median 6%, about $153), with the pre-event price regained in 12 to 50 sessions.
- **32 leadership-scandal and regulatory episodes at large companies.** From the day-5 close, the median further fall to the lowest close was 8% within 60 sessions ($149) and 25% within 250 sessions ($123); the bad quartile was 19% ($131) and 40% ($98). Dated from the CEO's resignation rather than the start of the board inquiry, the 60-session median is 16% ($136).
- **Where they ended, not just where they bottomed.** The median analog finished above its day-5 close after both 60 and 250 sessions, and 22 of 32 regained the pre-event price within a year.

### Price structure

- $148.80 (Thursday's low) and $149.43 (the January 2025 peak); then the heaviest volume band below the price at $130–145 (16% of two-year volume); then $115–125 and the 200-week average at $123.60; then the April 2025 low ($94.31 close, $86.62 intraday).
- These are chart markers, not support. About 58% of the last two years' volume traded above today's price, so most recent buyers are under water.
- The largest put open interest is at the $150 strike. Open interest does not show who is long or short, so it is positioning, not a floor.

### What argues the low is near

- Thursday's turnover ($124B) was the largest dollar volume in the ten-year record, and the stock closed 11.7% above its low.
- In 25 earnings reports since November 2020 the worst next-session close was −8.5%; a Wednesday close below $148.80 would be the worst in that record.
- The day-5 close was within 5% of the three-month low in 41% of the analog episodes.
- Consensus for next fiscal year is still rising, and the lowest analyst targets ($170–174) are above the price.

Against that: short interest is only 1.2% of float and headlines describe heavy retail and leveraged-fund dip buying, so positioning has not capitulated.

## What Tuesday can change

- Revenue against the $108.0B guide and $108.9B consensus. A January-quarter guide 10% below consensus maps to about $144; withheld guidance would widen the range.
- The first company disclosure of subpoenas, inquiries, the new class actions or a loss accrual. The only filing so far, the 8-K of 2026-11-12, confirms the CEO's resignation effective 2026-11-11 and gives no reason; the FBI, DOJ and congressional items are press reports.
- Charges on inventory or supply commitments, and marks on equity investments.
- Whether the buyback continues ($99.3B of authorisation remained in July).
- Any sign of a control or timing problem; the interim CEO is also the CFO.

## How this was checked, and its limits

- Four analysts worked independently, one per method. A fifth re-derived 35 key figures from raw sources: 29 confirmed, 5 differed in wording or definition, and 1 was wrong (an earlier draft called 17.0x a record-low multiple; it is a ten-year low). A sixth attacked the conclusions from both directions, and this version reflects that critique.
- Option odds are risk-neutral and from Friday's close; they will reprice on Monday. NVDA's own history in similar windows gives no sign the near-dated odds are inflated.
- The earnings-at-risk prices hold the multiple constant and the valuation markers hold earnings constant; a real downturn moves both, and applying an earnings cut at today's already-depressed multiple can double-count.
- The analog set is small, its event dates were recalled and validated only against the price reaction, and delisted scandal cases are missing, which understates the tail.
- All news items are headlines, not article text. The short-seller figures are allegations.

This is an analysis of what stated assumptions imply, not a forecast and not investment advice.
